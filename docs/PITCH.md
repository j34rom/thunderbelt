# Pitch — Thunderbelt Recycling Schedule (working title)

Three minutes, spoken. If there is more than one presenter, split the
beats between you and mark who says what.

## The script

<!-- script-start -->
### 1. The person

I finished a bottle of water, and I stood there holding it. Where does
it go? I had no idea. So I did what I always do. I threw it down
somewhere, or dropped it in the one bin we have.

I'm a student. I live in a residential area with almost no waste
management. This isn't a story about someone else. It's me, and it
happens every time I finish a drink.

### 2. Today

Right now, everything goes into one bin, and we wait for the garbage
truck. It comes on a random day. Sometimes there's a drop-off, but the
dates keep changing. Nobody I know can tell me who runs it, or when the
next one is.

So recycling just doesn't happen. Not because we don't want to, but
because we never know when and where.

### 3. Our fix

Our fix is a simple website you open on your phone. You pick your area,
and you see the next pick-up and drop-off for it. What, where, and when.

No account. No sign-up. We add the schedules ourselves, so there is one
place to look, and every entry shows when it was last updated.

### 4. The demo moment

It's already live, so I'll show you. We add a drop-off for Saturday at
two in the afternoon, in our area. Then we open the site on a phone, pick
the area, and there it is, right at the top, with the date, the time,
and the place.

### 5. How we'll know

Before Session 3, our relatives and neighbors will try it on their own
phones. We'll ask them one question: did you know when and where to take
your recycling? Then we'll tell you exactly what they said.
<!-- script-end -->

## Hard questions

**1. "Is this a real problem, or just your problem?"**
This is the weakest point. Right now the evidence is one person: you.
The honest answer: "It's our own experience, and we're the first user.
Before Session 3 we're going to ask neighbors and relatives whether they
have the same problem." Then bring their answers. Only say you have
already asked if you really have.

**2. "If nobody knows who runs the schedule, where will your data come
from? A wrong date is worse than no date."**
The honest answer: "We don't know yet. We plan to ask the barangay hall
who runs it. Until then we only enter dates we've confirmed ourselves,
and every entry shows the date it was last updated." Do not promise
official data you don't have, and do not say you have asked the barangay
until you have.

**3. "Why a website? Why not a poster or a group chat?"**
In the interview you said you weren't sure about this. A good answer:
"The dates keep changing, so a printed poster goes out of date, and
group chats bury old messages. One website, updated in one place, always
shows the latest." Be ready to admit that a poster could work for a
fixed schedule.

**4. "What stops someone from posting a fake schedule?"**
The honest answer: "Residents can only read. Only signed-in admins on our
team can add, edit or delete a collection, and the database itself
refuses anyone else, not just the page." This is true, and we tested it.

**5. "Why no incentives or points?"**
The honest answer: "We want them eventually, but points need a real
partner business to honor them and a way to confirm drop-offs. We have
neither yet, so we built the part that helps people today, knowing when
and where. Incentives are next." Point to the later list in
`docs/LATER.md`.

## Strength check

The evidence is thin. It rests on one person's experience, and the
schedule's source is unknown. Before you present, try to:
1. Ask three to five neighbors the same questions: where do you put your
   bottle, and do you know when the next drop-off is? Write down the
   answers.
2. Spend 10 minutes at the barangay hall asking who runs pick-up and
   drop-off, and how changes are announced.

Even two or three real answers make beat 1 and beat 5 much stronger.

## Word count

The script is about 275 words, which is roughly two minutes at a calm
speaking pace. That leaves room for pauses, and for showing the
mock-up if you have one. The limit is 450.
